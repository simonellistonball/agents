# Stage reference: Established private / subsidiary

## Emphasis

Established private companies are often opaque — no quarterly filings, limited analyst coverage, but a long track record to interpret. The research weighting shifts:

- **History and record: 25%** — long trajectory matters, pivots and acquisitions reveal strategy
- **Ownership: 20%** — who actually controls the company? PE-owned? Family-owned? ESOP?
- **Product and strategy: 20%** — mature products, but what's the latest strategic move?
- **Financials: 15%** — filed accounts (Companies House for UK, equivalent elsewhere), industry estimates
- **Negative sentiment: 10%** — mature companies accumulate more public controversies
- **Competitive landscape: 5%** — established market position, usually well-defined
- **Team: 5%** — C-suite only, unless founders still involved

## Source priorities

1. **Companies House (UK) / state registrar filings (US) / equivalent elsewhere** — for directors, ownership, filed accounts. In the UK, look at the `persons-with-significant-control` filings.
2. **Trade press** — often more detailed than tier-1 press for mid-market private companies
3. **Industry analyst reports** — Gartner, Forrester, IDC mentions; even just the fact of being mentioned tells you something
4. **Company press releases and investor pages** — if they have them (private companies sometimes do for bond investors)
5. **LinkedIn** — for headcount trajectory and C-suite tenure
6. **Acquisition databases** — tracking both acquisitions they've made and whether they've been acquired
7. **Bond prospectuses** — if the company has issued debt publicly, the prospectus is a goldmine
8. **Court records** — Pacer (US), BAILII (UK) — for litigation history

## Ownership — go deeper than "private"

"Private" can mean many things. Identify which:

- **Founder-owned / family-owned** — who are the family members? Succession plans?
- **Employee-owned (ESOP / co-op)** — rare but important for culture
- **Private equity owned** — which fund? When acquired? Typical PE hold is 5–7 years; where are they in that cycle?
- **Venture-backed but late-stage** — still has VC investors on the cap table, likely pre-IPO
- **Subsidiary of a larger entity** — research the parent, too. Create a `[[Parent Company]]` stub.
- **Founder / senior management buyout** — signals management confidence

For PE-owned companies specifically, note the buyout date, reported price, and any subsequent dividend recapitalisations (which often load the company with debt).

## Financial signals without filings

Even without audited public financials, you can triangulate:

- **Companies House micro-entity or abbreviated accounts** (UK) — limited but shows turnover bands and employee count
- **Press reports of revenue** — often reported at funding or acquisition events, or when execs give interviews
- **Employee count × industry revenue-per-employee** — rough estimate, note the method
- **Job postings** — volume and seniority signals growth or contraction

State the methodology explicitly for any estimated figure. Don't present estimates as facts.

## History — timeline approach

For established companies, a dated timeline is usually the clearest way to present history:

```markdown
## Timeline

- **YYYY** — Founded by X and Y in [city]
- **YYYY-MM** — Acquired [[Company]] for $Xm
- **YYYY-MM** — Launched [product line]
- **YYYY-MM** — Took on $Xm debt financing from [[Lender]]
- **YYYY-MM** — CEO transition: [Old CEO] → [New CEO]
```

Wikilink every person and company in the timeline.

## Competitors — include incumbents and insurgents

For an established company, competitive threat often comes from two directions:

1. **Peer incumbents** — the other established players in the same category
2. **Insurgent startups** — new entrants trying to disrupt with a different model

Make sure both groups appear in the landscape. An established company dossier that only lists other established competitors misses the most interesting signal.
