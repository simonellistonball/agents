# Stage reference: Startup (pre-seed / seed / Series A–B / scaleup)

## Emphasis

For early-stage companies, **the team is the thesis**. Most of what's knowable is in the founders and core team — there's limited product track record, limited financial history, and no analyst coverage. Weight accordingly:

- **Team: 30%** of the dossier — backgrounds, prior exits, why they're well-suited to this problem
- **Product and traction: 25%** — what's actually shipped, any customer logos, any reported metrics
- **Funding: 20%** — investors, round sizes, valuation trajectory, runway signals
- **Market and strategy: 15%** — TAM, positioning, moat thesis
- **Negative sentiment: 5%** — but don't shrink this just because the company is young; founder controversies and early-stage fraud are real risks
- **Competitive landscape: 5%** — breadth matters more than depth here

## Source priorities

1. **Crunchbase, PitchBook, Dealroom** — for funding. Often paywalled; look for aggregator summaries and press coverage of rounds.
2. **LinkedIn** — for team. Cross-reference founder claims against LinkedIn employment dates.
3. **The company's own blog and changelog** — what have they actually shipped, in what order?
4. **Twitter/X and LinkedIn posts from founders** — often the richest signal on strategy and culture
5. **Podcast appearances** — founders often say much more in 60-minute podcasts than in curated press
6. **Y Combinator / Techstars / accelerator pages** — if relevant
7. **Product Hunt, Hacker News** — early user reception
8. **Companies House (UK) or state filings (US)** — for directors, registered address, and filed accounts (note: early-stage accounts are often minimal)

## Team research (Pass G) — required for this stage

For each founder and up to 8 core team members, produce a short paragraph covering:

- **Name and current role**
- **Career arc** — name the companies and roles, most recent first, going back to education. Not just "ex-Google" — say "Staff Engineer at Google Search from 2019–2023, before that Senior Engineer at Stripe".
- **Education** — institution and subject, if discoverable
- **Prior exits or notable outcomes** — any companies founded, any acquired, any IPO'd
- **Prior failures** — if a previous venture shut down, note it factually. This is signal, not stigma.
- **Domain credibility** — publications, talks, open-source contributions, patents relevant to what the company is doing
- **Relationship to other founders** — worked together before? Co-authors? Classmates? This matters for team cohesion.

Wikilink every named company and investor — `[[Stripe]]`, `[[Y Combinator]]`, etc. — so they become stub pages.

## Funding specifics

For each funding round, produce a row:

| Date | Round | Amount | Lead investor(s) | Other participants | Post-money valuation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

If valuation isn't disclosed, say "undisclosed" — don't guess.

For each lead investor, note the firm briefly in a "Key investors" subsection. This is where `[[Sequoia]]`, `[[Andreessen Horowitz]]` wikilinks become useful.

## Traction signals to hunt for

- Announced customers or customer logos (usually on the website)
- ARR/MRR numbers (rare at early stage, but sometimes disclosed in funding announcements)
- Download counts (for consumer apps)
- GitHub stars / npm downloads (for dev tools)
- Community size (Discord, Slack community member counts)
- Hiring velocity — comparing LinkedIn headcount over time

Be explicit about the source of any traction number, because they're often self-reported.

## Red flags specific to early-stage

In addition to the general negative-sentiment pass, look for:

- **Founder churn** — co-founder left within 18 months of founding
- **Pivots without explanation** — especially if the original thesis was publicly announced
- **Inflated claims** — if the company says "AI-powered" but the team has no ML background, flag it
- **Undisclosed down rounds** — valuation declining between rounds is often not announced; look for signals
- **Litigation** — even small lawsuits are proportionally more significant at this stage
- **Regulatory exposure** — especially for fintech, healthtech, biotech
