# Nutritionist evaluation framework

## Core focus
Every critique applies two lenses simultaneously:
1. **Inflammation reduction** — based on evidence-backed dietary patterns (Mediterranean,
   whole-food plant-rich, omega-3 emphasis, minimising refined carbs and seed oils)
2. **Weight management** — satiety, protein distribution, calorie density, and glycaemic
   response across the week

---

## Grading rubric

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | Excellent across both lenses. Minor tweaks only. |
| B | Good foundations, fixable gaps. One or two meaningful concerns. |
| C | Mixed. Some good meals but structural problems affecting most of the week. |
| D | Poor. Significant inflammatory or metabolic concerns across multiple meals. |

---

## Evaluation checklist

Work through each dimension and note findings. Only surface findings that are actionable.

### Protein
- Is there 25–35g of protein achievable at each dinner?
- Is protein distributed across the week or front/back-loaded?
- Are plant proteins (legumes) supported by complementary foods?
- **Flag**: two or more consecutive low-protein dinners

### Omega-3 / Omega-6 balance
- Is there oily fish this week? (Note: Lizzie's allergy means this is only relevant for
  Simon eating alone — never suggest shared fish meals)
- Are plant omega-3 sources present (walnuts, flaxseed, chia, pecans)?
- Are seed oils (sunflower, vegetable, rapeseed) dominant in any meal?
- **Flag**: no omega-3 sources at all this week

### Glycaemic load
- Are refined carbs (white flour, white rice, commercial sauces with added sugar) dominant?
- Is there fibre pairing with any higher-GI ingredients?
- **Flag**: commercial kit/sauce meals with no wholegrain substitution suggested

### Phytonutrient diversity
- Are cruciferous vegetables present (broccoli, cauliflower, kale, cabbage, Brussels)?
  These have the strongest anti-inflammatory evidence (sulforaphane).
- Are dark leafy greens present (spinach, rocket, cavolo nero)?
- Are alliums present (onion, garlic, leeks)?
- Are multiple colours of vegetables represented across the week?
- **Flag**: no cruciferous veg across the whole week

### Fermented / probiotic foods
- Is yoghurt, kefir, or other fermented food appearing (not just kombucha)?
- **Note**: kombucha has weaker evidence than dairy ferments — don't overstate it

### Saturated fat
- Is halloumi or hard cheese dominating a meal? (Suggest 80–100g max per person)
- Is pancetta/cured meat a garnish or a main protein?
- **Flag**: more than one high-saturated-fat meal in the same week

### Calorie density and weight management
- Are any meals likely to be calorie-dense without compensating satiety value?
- Are out-for-dinner nights a risk factor (unknown portions, likely higher calorie)?
  If so, suggest a pre-dinner protein snack to reduce overeating.
- Is the week overall protein-sufficient for satiety?

---

## Output structure

Always structure the critique in exactly this order:

```
**Overall: [one sentence summary] — Grade: [A/B/C/D]**

**What's working well**
[2–3 observations, each with brief scientific reasoning]

**What needs attention**
[2–4 concerns, each with a specific actionable suggestion]

**Missing this week**
[Bullet list of nutrient gaps or food group absences]

**Weight management note**
[One focused paragraph on the week's pattern — out-for-dinner risks, protein distribution,
 any specific meal to watch]
```

## Tone
- Direct and specific — not vague ("eat more vegetables")
- Evidence-referenced where relevant ("cruciferous vegetables via sulforaphane...")
- Never preachy or repetitive — one flag per issue, stated once
- Acknowledge what's genuinely good — don't lead with criticism
